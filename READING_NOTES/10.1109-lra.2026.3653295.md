## Research question and assumptions

The paper asks how to preserve robot posture and maintain continuous, differentiable joint trajectories when a prescribed task-space path on a non-cuspidal robot must pass through a singularity and move between Uniqueness Domains (UDs). The mathematical method is developed for a UR-like 6R serial architecture with closed-form IK. A UR5e pick-and-place task is reported for a shoulder-singularity case. The paper conditions its continuity claim on the prescribed trajectory being kinematically admissible.

## IK representation and branch-transition method

The closed-form IK yields up to eight discrete solutions indexed by $(i,j,k)\in\{-1,1\}^3$. Three factors $P_1,P_2,P_3$ in the Jacobian determinant identify the elbow, wrist, and shoulder singularity families (Sec. II-A/B, Eqs. 13–14). Posture labels and branch indices are tied to the signs of those factors.

At a sampled pose, if $|P_n|\leq\varepsilon_n$, the algorithm considers both signs of the associated branch index and retains the candidate with the smallest joint displacement from the previous selected configuration (Sec. III-B, Algorithm 1). The thresholds are motivated by a first-order variation bound using factor gradients, a maximum joint velocity, and controller sampling frequency (Eqs. 15–17). This is a discrete singularity-detection threshold; it is not a trajectory-level velocity constraint.

The wrist case $P_2=\sin q_5=0$ creates a finite internal four-bar mobility and infinitely many IK configurations under the closed-form equations. The method extrapolates one driving joint (for example, $q_6$) from at least two previous samples, closes the four-bar analytically for $q_2,q_3,q_4$, and chooses the closure closest to the previous configuration (Sec. III-B, Figs. 6 and 9). The paper explains that a branch-index change is required when the singular factor's behavior demands it; keeping a fixed index is not correct for every singularity crossing.

## Experiments and reported results

1. **Wrist-singularity passage:** The paper analyzes a task-space circular arc centered at the wrist center, where $q_5$ moves from $+\pi/3$ to $-\pi/3$ through $q_5=0$. Keeping the same branch index lets the pose path reach its endpoint but produces discontinuities in $q_2,q_3,q_4,q_6$; the proposed transition keeps the reported joint trajectories continuous (Sec. III-A, Figs. 7–8).
2. **Shoulder-singularity pick-and-place:** A UR5e follows three straight Cartesian segments, each executed over 5 s, between four stated waypoints while moving an object from a shelf to a table (Sec. IV-B, Fig. 12). This is the explicit hardware task surfaced in the article text.
3. **Solver comparison:** The paper compares its method with the built-in UR solver, prior posture/decision methods, IK-Fast, IK-Geo, TRAC-IK, and MATLAB Levenberg–Marquardt. Across 1,000 repeated trials, the authors report TRAC-IK failed to find a continuous solution in 38 Peg-in-Hole trials and 102 Pick&Place trials (Sec. IV-C).
4. **Computation time:** The two trajectories were each solved 100 times on an Intel Core i3-5005U, 8 GB RAM, MATLAB R2023b, Windows 10. The text reports a computation-time reduction while retaining continuity, but the indexed extraction did not expose exact Table III cell values; no speedup factor is asserted (Sec. IV-D, Table III).

These are author-reported simulations/solver runs and a UR5e hardware task. They are not an independent reproduction.

## Constraints, guarantees, and limitations

- **Branch × singularity:** Directly relevant. The paper gives an explicit branch-indexed IK representation and a procedure for crossing selected singularities.
- **Branch × constraints:** Not established. The reviewed method does not formulate joint-position bounds, collision/clearance, torque, or trajectory-level velocity/acceleration constraints. The use of maximum joint velocity to set a detection threshold does not certify dynamic feasibility.
- **Guarantee scope:** The authors claim continuous, differentiable transitions for kinematically admissible prescribed paths. The reviewed text does not state a general theorem establishing continuous-time whole-path feasibility under joint, collision, or dynamic constraints.
- **Authors' limitations:** Paths requiring motion along the lost degree of freedom need controlled deviations or another strategy. Infeasibility detection and online replanning are future work. The authors call for validation on additional robot models before asserting broader generality.
