## Research question and assumptions

The paper asks whether particle-swarm optimization combined with product-of-exponentials forward kinematics (SI-PoE) can produce sequential joint configurations for a 7-DoF arm when a Cartesian end-effector path and workspace obstacles are known in advance. Its objectives are end-effector position/orientation tracking, obstacle avoidance, low joint movement/effort, and finite-jerk motion. The model and experiment use the fixed-base, 7-revolute-joint Sawyer arm. Cartesian path samples are solved successively; this is not a closed-loop proof for every point of a continuous path.

## Method and actual continuity mechanism

Forward kinematics is written in PoE form from the Sawyer URDF screw axes and home transforms. The SI-PoE search uses 15 particles in the seven joint coordinates: the previous successful joint configuration is the center, with individual particles offset by approximately ±1 rad in one joint coordinate. Particle velocities are random in [−0.1, 0.1]; the PSO update uses the reported parameters `ω=1`, `c1=2`, `c2=2.5`, and constriction `κ=0.5`. A point solve stops at 20 iterations or fitness below 0.0005.

The fitness combines Cartesian position error, optional orientation error, a weighted elementwise absolute displacement from the previous joint vector, and a collision indicator. The displacement weights favor proximal joints. Collision detection evaluates PoE positions of selected virtual link points against modeled obstacles; the indicator is +1 if any point collides and 0 otherwise. The method therefore encourages local joint motion by warm-starting from the previous sample and penalizing displacement. It does not define discrete IK branch labels, compare branch identities, or test/guarantee that a branch is preserved through singularity approach or crossing. The authors do not formulate hard joint-position, velocity, or acceleration inequalities in the SI-PoE search.

The Cartesian path is time-scaled with a quintic polynomial to give finite jerk at the end effector. A separate quintic polynomial finite-jerk (QPFJ) profile can interpolate a joint vector from initial to final positions. The paper's own comparison shows that applying QPFJ independently produces large Cartesian path error and may collide with obstacles; joining multiple QPFJ segments through SI-PoE via-points is suggested as future integration, with extra computation.

## Evaluation and results

- **Initialization comparison:** Table 1 reports averages over 30 runs: random versus proposed previous-solution-centered initialization took 0.24 versus 0.21 s for one-point IK and 13.61 versus 12.85 s for trajectory IK. Both hit the 20-iteration cap without meeting the stated fitness threshold; the paper reports fewer trajectory error spikes for the proposed initialization.
- **Simulation:** The example uses three spherical obstacles and eight virtual points (link centers of gravity). A moving-average filter takes about 0.02 s per 100 trajectory points. The authors report a maximum error reduction from about 13 mm to 4 mm at 10 s after filtering, while the reported integrated error rises from 0.0363 to 0.0440 (the PDF labels the unit “ms,” which is not a conventional position-error integral unit). A simulated obstacle avoidance example shows joint 5 being used to keep moving toward the task path.
- **Hardware:** Joint trajectories were sent to the fixed-base Sawyer through a ROS/Python SDK environment; measured joint positions, velocities, and torques were recorded. For the main 20 s motion, trajectory computation took about 13 s and maximum Cartesian error was reported around 6 mm. The measured torque profiles were compared with inverse-dynamics simulation; movement penalty reduced torque only marginally, around 2–3 Nm for most joints. Straight-line and constant-screw-axis trials demonstrate deliberate end-effector deviation around obstacles; the paper reports increased tracking error during those avoidance intervals.
- **Failure/limitation evidence:** Sparse virtual points can miss an obstacle between samples. Adding more points raises computation; inflating obstacle boundaries can shrink the search space, create undesirable paths, or eliminate solutions. QPFJ joint smoothing alone did not preserve simultaneous path tracking and obstacle avoidance.

## Relevance and evidence boundary

This is useful **adjacent** evidence for sequential trajectory IK on a fixed-base redundant serial arm: previous-solution initialization and a displacement penalty are practical continuity heuristics, and the authors validate sampled trajectories on hardware. It does not establish trajectory-consistent branch tracking: there is no branch identity, branch-switch decision, singularity transition analysis, cycle-closure condition, or continuity guarantee. The obstacle method is based on finitely sampled virtual points and is not a continuous swept-volume certificate. Joint movement is penalized, but hard joint-limit and rate/acceleration feasibility are not certified. Keep it as adjacent context on heuristic trajectory-level optimization and multiobjective tradeoffs, rather than as direct evidence for branch tracking or singularity transitions.
