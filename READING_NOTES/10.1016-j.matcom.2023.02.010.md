## Research question and contribution

The paper proposes a way to create smooth 3D end-effector paths through a set of via-points by expressing the geometry in cylindrical/spherical coordinates. It fits one quintic Hermite piecewise interpolant to radial distance as a function of azimuth and another to height in a rotating azimuthal plane. It then derives forward and inverse kinematics and inverse dynamics for a redundant serial arm and outlines optimization objectives for joint motion, end-effector error, power, and travel time.

The central contribution is the coordinate construction and its numerical demonstration. It is not an algorithm for labelling IK branches or proving that a joint-space solution continues on the same branch over a Cartesian trajectory.

## Robot, task, and kinematic assumptions

- The numerical model is fixed at the origin of the world reference frame and is serial. The example uses four links: a short vertical rotating base link (0.005 m), two arm links (0.2 m and 0.3 m), and an end-effector link (0.02 m). The mechanism has a base azimuth rotation and a three-link chain in the rotating plane.
- Tasks specify end-effector **positions** at via-points. The end-effector direction in the rotating plane, denoted by the absolute angle \(\Phi\), is free and supplies the redundancy used by the model. No full six-dimensional pose path is imposed.
- The numerical model uses steel links, with dimensions and inertial assumptions given in Sec. 4. The path input is a finite sequence of radial distances, azimuth angles, and heights.

## Path representation and IK calculation

The path is generated by quintic Hermite pieces: \(r(\theta)\) describes the projected radial path and \(z(\phi)\) describes height in the rotating azimuthal plane. Values, first derivatives, and second derivatives are assigned at the via-points. The construction yields a smooth interpolated task-space curve through the points. It does not define a permitted deviation corridor around a separately supplied continuous Cartesian path.

For a given path location and free end-effector angle \(\Phi\), Eq. (15) gives analytical joint angles \(\Phi_1,\Phi_2,\Phi_3\) for the three-link chain in the rotating plane. Joint rates are obtained from the inverse of the 3-by-3 Jacobian in Eqs. (11), (13), and (16). The paper also describes the inverse dynamics through the Lagrangian and multibody inertia model (Eqs. 17–21). The free angle \(\Phi\) is itself represented as a piecewise polynomial with zero angular velocity at the beginning and end of the task.

The discrete position-error objective in Eq. (23) is a weighted sum of end-effector position error, squared joint displacement, end-effector velocity, and absolute joint displacement; the weights sum to one. Section 3 also formulates a damped-least-squares (DLS) objective for reducing large joint rates. The damping factor is described as depending on the smallest singular value of the angular Jacobian, but the study reports \(\lambda=0.02\) throughout and gives no adaptive law or threshold. Minimum-power and minimum-time/mechanical-energy objectives are also written down (Eqs. 24–25); Eq. (25) states velocity, acceleration, and jerk constraints, but the paper does not give bound values or show a numerical constraint-verification study for them.

## Branch continuity, singularities, and joint constraints

- **Solution branch:** The analytical IK uses a free orientation angle and returns joint angles at points along the interpolated path. The paper does not define branch labels, enumerate and compare multiple solution branches, warm-start a continuation solver, or specify a branch-switch rule. Piecewise smoothness of \(\Phi\) alone does not establish continuity of a selected IK branch.
- **Singularities:** DLS is included as a local joint-rate regularizer, with a damping value reported. Example 1 discusses singularities at cusp vertices of an imagined tricuspid **task-space path**, where end-effector motion becomes very slow. The generated curve rounds those corners into semicircular segments instead of following the imagined curve between via-points. This is task-path smoothing; it is not a demonstrated crossing of a manipulator Jacobian singularity or a solution-branch transition. No Jacobian condition trace, singular-value margin, deliberate IK singularity experiment, or branch continuity result is reported.
- **Joint limits:** The paper does not impose or report joint-position bounds. Joint displacement and velocity terms appear in objectives, while a separate minimum-time formulation mentions velocity, acceleration, and jerk constraints without numerical bounds or a documented enforcement result. No torque-limit guarantee is established.
- **Collision and dynamics:** Link dynamics are modeled and torques are derived, but collision avoidance is not included in the three numerical cases. The paper does not report a hardware execution or a certified dynamic-feasibility result.

## Evaluation and results

The examples are Matlab numerical simulations of the four-link model. Example 1 uses via-points at vertices of an imagined tricuspid path; the generated end-effector curve smooths the cusp corners rather than following the drawn reference shape between via-points. Example 2 uses mostly circular, planar via-points. Example 3 uses ten irregular 3D via-points and plots end-effector and joint trajectories, the second-joint optimized trajectory, and the relative angles of links 2 and 3. Figure 7 shows continuous plotted projections of end-effector velocity in the \(xy\), \(xz\), and \(yz\) planes.

The evidence is qualitative and model-based. The paper reports no numerical end-effector error, joint-limit margin, branch-switch count, singularity metric, runtime benchmark, baseline comparison, or physical robot trial. The optimization section provides candidate objectives, but does not provide enough solver settings, objective weights, or constraint bounds to reproduce the reported optimized joint curve exactly.

## Relevance to the review

**Full-text assessment: retain the citation, but use it as adjacent/background evidence in synthesis.** It is useful for task-space smooth interpolation, analytical IK parameterized by a free redundant angle, and a DLS regularizer in trajectory generation. The fixed-base serial example overlaps the robot scope.

It does not directly establish trajectory-consistent branch tracking: the paper has no branch identity, continuity state, or branch-switch logic. Its cusp example concerns smoothing the Cartesian reference, not a verified manipulator singularity transition. It also lacks joint-position limits and does not report verified derivative-limit satisfaction. Do not cite it as evidence for those three review claims.
