# Full-text reading note — Redundancy parameterization and inverse kinematics of 7-DOF revolute manipulators

## Bibliographic identity and source

- Alexander Elias and John T. Wen, [arXiv:2307.13122v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13122), 2024 revision; DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2307.13122](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2307.13122). Preprint status is retained.
- Read the v2 arXiv PDF. No peer-reviewed Version of Record is claimed.

## Research question and assumptions

Develop redundancy parameters for families of fixed-base 7R revolute arms and provide IK solutions across those families. The focus is pose-to-configuration geometry, with example motions rather than a general constrained path planner.

## IK representation

The conventional shoulder-elbow-wrist (SEW) angle is generalized using a stereographic SEW parameterization. Fixing the redundancy parameter reduces 7R IK to 6R subproblems; depending on kinematic structure, solutions are closed form or use one-/two-dimensional search. A tangent-half-angle polynomial construction offers stronger all-root behavior at higher cost.

## Solution-branch treatment

The method enumerates multiple discrete branches at a chosen redundancy value. It demonstrates continuous parameter selection at a boundary singularity in examples, but does not define a general persistent branch planner with joint/collision feasibility constraints.

## Singularity handling

Stereographic SEW relocates the conventional line singularity to a half-line that may be positioned away from useful workspace; the paper also proves that a parameterization singularity cannot be eliminated globally. Specific examples adjust the redundancy value through boundary singularities. This is not a guarantee for arbitrary task paths or physical manipulator singularities.

## Constraints and executable feasibility

No general joint-position, velocity, acceleration, jerk, torque, obstacle, or clearance constraint set is enforced along a whole path. Pose solutions and example trajectories are not path-wide executability certification.

## Guarantees

Geometric results establish properties of the parameterization and solve IK for treated kinematic families. They do not prove continuous-time feasibility of arbitrary paths under robot constraints.

## Experiments and reported results

The paper gives IK examples across multiple 7R families and numerical trajectory demonstrations on KUKA LBR iiwa and UR5 models. A Sawyer polynomial example reaches degree 48 and returns 16 real candidates, 7 valid under the original pose equation (Sec. 6, Table 4). Evidence is computational/demonstrative, not physical validation.

## Authors' limitations

Future work includes trajectory design for the new stereographic parameterization and additional 7R families.

## Evidence-bounded TCIK classification

Strong background for redundancy parameterization, branch multiplicity, and algorithmic singularities. It does not establish branch × singularity × hard-constraint whole-path feasibility.
