# Full-text reading note — Meta-Heuristics Based Inverse Kinematics of Robot Manipulator’s Path Tracking Capability Under Joint Limits

## Bibliographic identity and source

- G. Kanagaraj and Vincent Yu, MENDEL 2022, DOI [10.13164/mendel.2022.1.041](https://doi.org/10.13164/mendel.2022.1.041).
- Read the publisher Version of Record PDF at [MENDEL](https://mendel-journal.org/index.php/mendel/article/download/173/176), labelled publishedVersion in the source ledger.

## Research question and assumptions

Compare population metaheuristics for tracking position-only Cartesian paths of a fixed-base Yaskawa MH5 six-axis arm under joint-angle limits. Orientation is not prescribed; four curve trajectories use 17 Cartesian points each.

## IK representation

At each waypoint the paper optimizes Cartesian RMS position error with BAT, GSA, PSO, and WOA. The previous waypoint's joint solution is reused as the next initialization/reference.

## Solution-branch treatment

The warm start encourages small joint changes empirically, but the method does not label IK branches, prove branch identity, or specify branch transitions. Previous-solution initialization is not a branch-tracking guarantee.

## Singularity handling

There is no singularity-crossing method. The authors state joint limits prevent singularities in their tests; this is an empirical claim about the tested configurations, not controlled passage through singularities.

## Constraints and executable feasibility

Joint-angle position limits are imposed. The studied formulation does not include obstacles/collision, clearance, velocity, acceleration, jerk, torque, or dynamic constraints. It does not certify continuous feasibility between the 17 Cartesian samples.

## Guarantees

Results are numerical/metaheuristic outcomes. There is no proof of global optimizer convergence, continuous path lifting, or a bound on inter-sample joint motion.

## Experiments and reported results

Four paths × four algorithms × 20 runs are reported, with population 60 and up to 100 iterations (Secs. 4.1–4.4). BAT has the lowest reported Cartesian error; GSA gives lower joint variation on some comparisons. Wilcoxon comparisons and convergence behavior appear in tables/figures. No physical robot trial is reported.

## Authors' limitations

The conclusion identifies obstacles and hybrid optimization approaches as future work. The evaluation is MATLAB simulation with sampled position-only targets.

## Evidence-bounded TCIK classification

Supporting background on pointwise metaheuristic IK with joint-position bounds. A previous solution is used for initialization, but persistent branch identity and continuous-time guarantees are not established.
