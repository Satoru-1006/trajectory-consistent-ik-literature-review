# Full-text reading note — Dynamic Programming-Based Offline Redundancy Resolution of Redundant Manipulators Along Prescribed Paths with Real-Time Adjustment

## Bibliographic identity and source

- Zhihang Yin, Fa Wu, Ziqiang Wang, Jianmin Yang, Jiyong Tan, and Dexing Kong, [arXiv:2411.17052v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17052), revised 18 March 2025. DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2411.17052](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2411.17052); preprint status retained.
- Full text read from the arXiv HTML version.

## Research question and assumptions

Resolve redundancy for a prescribed discrete Cartesian path under joint limits and a velocity bound, then adjust the path online when the current configuration cannot track it. The principal redundant platform is a fixed-base 7-DOF Franka arm.

## IK representation

Analytical Franka IK parameterizes q7; dynamic programming selects a sequence over finite q7 and normal-direction path-offset grids. The reported global maximum step parameter applies over those sampled states, not a continuous decision space.

## Solution-branch treatment

The solver evaluates multiple analytic IK branches and links configurations along the discretized path. Nodes at singular configurations are excluded by a Jacobian test; the method avoids singular nodes rather than controlling branch transitions through them.

## Singularity handling

Singular configurations are screened out. No singularity crossing or branch transition through a singular set is demonstrated.

## Constraints and executable feasibility

Joint position and velocity constraints are included in discrete planning. A real-time adjustment parameter translates the task path along the end-effector z normal. Acceleration/jerk are addressed by a downstream 1 kHz motion-compensation controller, which can move the realized end effector away from the prescribed path. No obstacle/collision constraint is included.

## Guarantees

Dynamic programming gives an optimum/maximum over finite sampled path, q7, and offset grids. It does not prove continuous-path feasibility or continuous clearance. Motion compensation is evaluated empirically and can affect path accuracy.

## Experiments and reported results

The authors evaluate simulated paths and a Franka hardware task with 101 task samples and 0.1 s waypoint timing. The proposed method completes a task for which the built-in pose generator stops near the joint-7 limit; reported real-time end-effector errors are around 10^-3 m. Joint velocity/acceleration/jerk are addressed, including motion compensation (Secs. 4–5).

## Authors' limitations

The paper reports motion deviation from the intended path due to compensation and notes that complex paths, frequent direction changes, and acceleration/deceleration can cause error or vibration.

## Evidence-bounded TCIK classification

Substantial counterexample to treating redundancy resolution as pose-local only: it combines discrete branch selection, q/qd bounds, prescribed-path planning, real-time adjustment, and hardware. Singularities are avoided and collision is absent, so it does not establish branch transition through singularities or full constrained continuous-path feasibility.
