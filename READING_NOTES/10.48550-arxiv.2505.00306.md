# Full-text reading note — J-PARSE: Jacobian-based Projection Algorithm for Resolving Singularities Effectively in Inverse Kinematic Control of Serial Manipulators

## Bibliographic identity and source

- Shivani Guptasarma, M. Strong, Honghao Zhen, and Monroe Kennedy, [arXiv:2505.00306v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00306), revised 22 September 2026; DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2505.00306](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2505.00306). Preprint.
- Read the complete v6 arXiv PDF.

## Research question and assumptions

Stabilize first-order Cartesian velocity IK for fixed-base serial manipulators near workspace/internal singularities, including target poses on or outside workspace boundaries. The paper studies 2R models and physical X-Arm7 and Kinova Gen3 robots.

## IK representation

An SVD “Safety Jacobian” floors small singular values at a fraction γ of the maximum, separates singular and nonsingular task directions, scales commands in singular directions, and applies a right inverse in the remaining directions. A projected null-space potential can bias posture toward a nominal configuration.

## Solution-branch treatment

This is local velocity control, not a global branch planner. A nominal posture potential can favor an elbow-up configuration in examples, but context-aware branch-exit selection and redundancy-wide stability are left open.

## Singularity handling

The method enables stable approach to and departure from near-boundary/internal singular configurations under stated assumptions. Exact singular starts are treated separately; motion in a lost task direction can remain unavailable. This is local singularity-robust control, not global branch transition.

## Constraints and executable feasibility

The stability/velocity analysis is explicitly scoped without joint limits and collision constraints. Joint-position feasibility, self/external collision, full path planning, and global branch selection are not enforced. The projected potential is not a hard-constraint certificate.

## Guarantees

The paper gives local stability and local asymptotic-stability results for stated nonsingular-start assumptions, including near-singular operation, with a sufficient discrete-time step bound and a velocity bound depending on γ. These are local controller guarantees, not whole-path realization under joint/collision constraints.

## Experiments and reported results

Simulations and physical X-Arm7/Kinova Gen3 demonstrations include teleoperation/visual-servo motions near boundary or internal singular configurations. A diffusion-policy task reports fetching success in 9/10 trials (Secs. VIII–X). Tests show local behavior, not hard-constrained path planning.

## Authors' limitations

The paper leaves feasible-path identification under collision and joint limits, context-aware branch selection, and broader redundancy analysis to future work. It also notes acceleration discontinuity at the threshold while arguing it does not invalidate the stated stability result.

## Evidence-bounded TCIK classification

Strong adjacent evidence for singularity-robust local control, including hardware. It shows singularity handling exists, but does not resolve branch identity or joint/collision-constrained path feasibility.
