# Full-text reading note — Path Planning and Optimization for Cuspidal 6R Manipulators

## Bibliographic identity and source

- Alexander J. Elias and John T. Wen, [arXiv:2501.18505v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18505), 29 April 2025; DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2501.18505](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2501.18505). Preprint.
- Read the complete v2 arXiv PDF. Demonstrations use software/simulation environments; no physical robot trial is reported.

## Research question and assumptions

Determine whether a prescribed workspace path admits a continuous joint-space realization on cuspidal 6R arms, where pose-wise IK feasibility does not imply a continuous lift. Examples include ABB GoFa, FANUC CRX, ABB IRB6640, and three-parallel-axis families.

## IK representation

Compute IK solutions at samples along the Cartesian path, build a directed acyclic graph whose nodes are sampled solutions, connect adjacent candidates when wrapped joint change meets a threshold, and solve a shortest-path problem for a chosen cost such as finite-difference joint velocity. An outer derivative-free optimizer shifts the toolpath in the robot base frame and reruns the graph planner.

## Solution-branch treatment

Explicitly enumerates multiple IK solutions per sample and links them into a discrete solution graph. This is direct path-level branch selection. Branch identity is induced by thresholded edges, not a persistent analytic label.

## Singularity handling

The planner can seek nonsingular paths by rejecting determinant-sign changes, or permit graph paths through singularities by removing that restriction. Approximate/least-squares IK supports workspace boundary/internal singular cases. The discrete graph does not prove continuous singularity passage between every linked sample.

## Constraints and executable feasibility

Sampled hard/soft joint-position and derivative constraints (including velocity/acceleration-related edge limits) are available in path selection. Collision checks for the end effector and every link are not included in the reported optimizer; the authors list this as future work. No full time-parameterized dynamic or clearance certificate is established.

## Guarantees

Shortest-path optimality is over the finite graph and sampled cost. Outer optimization is local derivative-free search, with multiple-minimum sensitivity. Neither stage certifies continuous-time, collision-free constrained execution of the exact Cartesian curve.

## Experiments and reported results

Numerical/software demonstrations include GoFa reconfiguration, CRX MoveL feasibility dependent on endpoint branches, an in-workspace infeasible IRB6640 path, and toolpath-placement tests with about 500 samples. Joint-motion objectives improve in examples; multiple local minima motivate multiple starts. No hardware experiment is reported (Secs. III–V and figures).

## Authors' limitations

Future work includes collision checks for every link/end effector, time-optimal objectives, and improved sampling/analytic gradients. Toolpath optimization is local and paths are sampled.

## Evidence-bounded TCIK classification

Potentially Core-level evidence on branch selection along prescribed paths and singularity-permitting transitions, with sampled joint/derivative constraints. It is the strongest counterexample in this batch to a categorical claim that no method combines these axes. Graph discretization, threshold edges, optional singularity logic, absent collision constraints, and no continuous-time proof keep the finding bounded; the frozen screening label remains Adjacent.
