## Problem and formulation

The paper asks how to keep optimization-based IK feasible when joint position, velocity, acceleration, self-collision, and obstacle-avoidance inequalities interact. The target task is expressed by task-space error `e(q)` and Jacobian `J(q)`; the differential IK objective tracks `J qdot ≈ e/τ` while penalizing unnecessary joint velocity. The state is `[q, qdot]` and acceleration is the control input. The discrete-time model uses trapezoidal position integration and sampled acceleration updates.

For geometry, the original joint-range and collision set is approximated by a set of linear “compound joint constraints” `Aq ≤ q̃_lim`, combined with box velocity and acceleration limits. The paper defines an auxiliary viability set for the discrete-time double integrator. In one dimension, a braking-distance condition prevents a joint from reaching a position boundary at a velocity from which it cannot stop. For each row of `A`, the method extends that construction using a conservative effective deceleration. Multiple rows produce a joint viability set, which is enforced in a QP at each sample.

The method has two stages. Offline, mixed-integer linear programming, polytope-vertex enumeration, and a QP calculate conservative constants `ã_lim`. Online, the constraints are updated from the current state and one QP computes `qdot_k`. The paper’s lemma gives a one-step invariant-set condition; under the stated discrete-time model and approximated constraints, if the state starts in the constructed viable set, a feasible next acceleration/velocity exists while remaining in the set. This is a discrete-time state-constraint guarantee, not a continuous-time or arbitrary Cartesian path-lift certificate.

## Branches, singularities, and applicability to trajectory IK

The method has no persistent IK branch label, branch continuation procedure, branch-switching decision, or singularity-crossing analysis. Redundancy is resolved through the weighted differential IK objective; no analytic solution families are tracked. Although it directly treats physical joint range, velocity and acceleration constraints and whole-body collision inequalities, it does not study whether a prescribed Cartesian path admits an uninterrupted IK branch, nor prove an end-effector tracking error bound through singularity transitions.

The authors assume a sample interval `T`; feasibility and the braking envelope depend on it. Joint and collision geometry are represented through a conservative linear polyhedral approximation. The guarantee therefore applies to that discrete system and that approximation. It does not certify unsampled continuous motion or eliminate modeling error in the collision-distance approximation.

## Evaluation

The first simulation uses a one-DOF system, position limit `q ≤ 1`, velocity limit 3, acceleration limit 12, a target at 1.1 (outside the allowable range), and compares the proposed construction with two prior viability approximations at `T=0.01 s` and `T=0.1 s`. The paper reports that the proposed set has better approximation accuracy across these sampling intervals and avoids the oscillatory acceleration observed for one comparator; the other comparator is more conservative.

The second simulation uses a planar 2-DOF arm with link lengths 1 m, joint ranges `[0,0]` to `[π,π/2]`, velocity bounds `[1,2]`, acceleration bounds `[15,12]`, obstacles, and a four-segment target-position sequence over 4 s. Figures 6–7 show simulated obstacle avoidance and joint-state constraint satisfaction. This is a numerical illustration, not a hardware experiment or continuous collision verification.

The offline-construction timing study generates compound constraints with `m=2n` for `n=2…10`, then records mean times for MILP, vertex enumeration, and QP, each over 15 runs. Total time rises from 0.0061 s at `(n,m)=(2,4)` to 1.080 s at `(7,128)`, 120.24 s at `(9,512)`, and 1563.09 s at `(10,1024)`, with vertex enumeration dominating at high dimension. The authors also say smaller constraint counts allow some `n=20` cases. No online QP timing table or robot experiment is reported in the inspected manuscript.

## Limitations and review assessment

The authors state that the viable state set can be conservative because (1) nonlinear geometric/collision constraints are replaced by linear compound inequalities and (2) fixed deceleration parameters are chosen from worst cases. They also state that the method is not sufficiently general for some large sampling intervals and higher-dimensional cases. Suggested future work includes unions of compound constraints and dynamic adjustment of deceleration parameters.

**Relevance:** strong adjacent evidence for differential IK with hard joint-range, velocity, acceleration, and whole-body collision constraints; its discrete-time viability construction clarifies how feasibility preservation differs from pointwise QP constraint satisfaction. **Boundary:** it does not address solution-branch tracking, singularity transitions, or certified continuous Cartesian trajectory lifting; demonstrations are simulation-only and geometric constraints are approximate.
