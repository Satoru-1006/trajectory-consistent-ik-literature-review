## Research question and assumptions

The paper plans a Cartesian end-effector motion for a redundant 7-DOF serial arm while trading off obstacle clearance, joint velocity, jerk, and a torque/velocity-based cost. The illustrated arm has a fixed base. This is a simulation study, not a hardware evaluation. The environment contains one known, static spherical obstacle; the authors explicitly leave dynamic obstacles in unknown environments for future work.

The six end-effector coordinates are parameterized independently by a fifth-degree polynomial multiplied by a cosine term. Endpoint pose, zero endpoint velocity, and zero endpoint acceleration are prescribed. The resulting Cartesian path is described as helical. The optimizer varies six period/phase pairs, `ξ=[T1, φ1, …, T6, φ6]`, and the motion duration is fixed at 40 s in the reported example. The initial joint vector is all zeros, the joint bounds are ±100° for all seven joints, and the target pose is `[-1.50, 1.20, 3.60 m, 78°, −73°, −90°]` from `[-0.5, −0.2, 4.6 m, 0°, 0°, 0°]`.

## IK and trajectory method

The joint velocity is computed with a Jacobian pseudoinverse plus a null-space gradient term. The proposed joint-limit potential is `H(q)=Σ_i (q_i,max−q_i,min)/((q_i,max−q_i)(q_i−q_i,min))`; it is smallest at each joint midpoint and grows without bound at either limit. The paper substitutes `k∇H(q)` into the null-space term. It does not state the sign convention for `k` in the method text. With positive `k`, the displayed plus-gradient update appears to ascend this potential toward the limits; a negative sign or a different convention would be needed for descent. The sign is not clarified, which makes the stated joint-limit behavior difficult to reproduce from the equations alone.

The collision model envelopes arm links in cylinders and joints/obstacles in spheres, then computes segment-to-point distances in 3D. A positive minimum clearance denotes no collision. In the optimization, `f1=Σ_i |q̇_i| + obstacle penalty`; the authors say low joint velocity also has a singularity-avoidance effect, but give no singular-value/conditioning criterion, singularity set, transition rule, or proof. The inverse uses an ordinary Jacobian pseudoinverse involving `(JJᵀ)⁻¹`, with no stated damped or rank-deficient transition treatment.

The other objectives are `f2=Σ_i |q⃛_i|` and `f3=Σ_i ∫[q̇_i(t)τ_i(t)]²dt`. Although the paper calls `f3` total energy consumption, the stated functional is the time integral of squared mechanical power, not the usual work/energy integral `∫τᵀq̇ dt`; this distinction matters when interpreting the reported objective. Joint bounds also appear as `q_i,min ≤ q_i(t) ≤ q_i,max`, but the outer MOPSO decision variables are Cartesian trajectory periods/phases, not constrained joint samples. The bounds are handled through the null-space term rather than enforced as explicit inequalities in the evolutionary search, so the formulation does not establish hard boundedness for every continuous time instant.

MOPSO maintains an external Pareto archive, removes dominated particles, and invokes an adaptive-grid procedure when the archive reaches capacity. The reported population/archive sizes are 1000/1500, and `C1=C2=2.05`. Failed obstacle-avoidance particles can be removed. Since there is no single optimum, the authors compare one non-dominated solution selected for low jerk (`S2`) and one for low cost (`S3`).

## Evaluation and results

- **Simulation setup:** one 7-DOF arm, fixed initial/target poses, 40 s motion, joint bounds ±100°, link radius 0.1 m, joint envelope radius 0.1 m, and a sphere obstacle of radius 0.2 m centered at `[-0.5, 0.3, 1.25 m]`. No physical robot experiment is reported. The authors state that data are available from the corresponding author on request.
- **Pareto examples:** `S2` has objective vector `[0.19, 0.05, 2.08×10^5]`; `S3` has `[10.23, 58.08, 2.79×10^4]`. Their reported minimum clearances are 0.0597 m and 0.0014 m, respectively. Both reach the target pose in simulation, with different end-effector and joint paths.
- **Joint behavior:** `S2` is comparatively smooth. In `S3`, a joint approaches its bound near 24 s; the plotted joint velocity changes abruptly, followed by a roughly 2 s shaking/settling interval. The authors interpret this as the null-space term returning the joint to the allowed range. It also shows that limit avoidance can induce a large transient and does not provide a smooth transition guarantee.
- **Figures checked:** the robot/envelope geometry and archive flow (Figs. 1–5), convergence and Pareto plots (Figs. 6–7), clearance and configurations (Figs. 8–9), and end-effector/joint trajectories and velocities (Figs. 10–12) were visually compared with the text and tabulated results.

## Limitations and relevance to this review

After full-text review, this is **adjacent evidence** for the proposed review. It contributes a Cartesian-space multi-objective trajectory optimizer and an example of null-space joint-limit handling for a fixed-base redundant serial arm. It does not identify discrete IK solution branches, track branch labels, analyze branch changes, or demonstrate a controlled passage through a singular configuration. Differential pseudoinverse integration may yield a locally evolving joint path in the simulation, but the paper does not formulate or guarantee trajectory-consistent branch continuation. It also has no joint-space cycle-closure condition.

The claims are bounded by a single static spherical obstacle, a simplified envelope collision model, one simulated arm/task, and no real-robot or uncertainty evaluation. The reported 1.4 mm minimum clearance of `S3` is especially sensitive to geometry/model error. The velocity-minimization claim for singularity avoidance is not supported by a singularity metric, and the Jacobian inverse is undefined at rank loss. The equation sign ambiguity in the joint-limit potential and the difference between the named energy cost and its written functional should be resolved before reusing either mechanism as a dependable guarantee.
