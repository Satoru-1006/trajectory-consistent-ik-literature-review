# Full-text reading note: Inverse Kinematics of Redundant Manipulators With Dynamic Bounds on Joint Movements

**Citation:** Marco Faroni, Manuel Beschi, Nicola Pedrocchi. *IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters* 5(4), 6435–6442 (2020). DOI: [10.1109/LRA.2020.3013879](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2020.3013879).

**Full-text source:** [Politecnico di Milano institutional repository PDF](https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/08ad5071-1478-4b96-96f2-1dff1e3cdac2/11311-1255958_Faroni.pdf). The nine-page PDF metadata and article text match the DOI/title/authors. Read on 2026-09-29.

## Research question and assumptions

The paper addresses online redundant IK that must follow a Cartesian path while remaining near a previously planned, collision-free joint-space path. A local differential IK controller can otherwise drift away from that path; ordinary hard bounds can also make its QP infeasible. The authors test a UR5 arm mounted on a motorized linear rail. The resulting mechanism has one prismatic rail coordinate plus six revolute arm joints, so the hardware evidence is not for a fixed-base serial manipulator. The method assumes a supplied nominal configuration trajectory `q_nom(t)`.

## Method

At each control step, a QP combines Cartesian path following as the primary task with secondary posture objectives. Joint position, velocity, and acceleration limits are included. A time-varying position tube enforces `q_nom(t) - Δq_max ≤ q(t) ≤ q_nom(t) + Δq_max`.

To keep that tube feasible under double-integrator joint dynamics and bounded velocity/acceleration, the paper derives viability constraints in position–velocity space. The exact viable boundary is parabolic; the implementation approximates it by a polygon with linear facets. The authors describe convergence toward the exact set as the number of facets increases. Task scaling `s ∈ [0,1]` slows progression along the Cartesian path while preserving its geometry. In the worst case, `s=0` freezes the nominal tube and maintains viability. A discrete PID adjusts the weight on the nominal-path task from the deviation relative to a reference value; anti-windup is included.

## Evidence and results

- **Simulation:** MoveIt BiTRRT-Connect supplies a collision-free nominal joint path. The Cartesian projection is the primary task and elbow clearance from a table is secondary. Four configurations compare unconstrained/adaptive weighting and hard-tube variants. Without the hard tube, the adaptive-weight case exceeds the prescribed deviation around 10.5 s; the combined hard-tube/task-scaling case remains within `q_nom ± Δq_max`. That case takes about 1.5 times as long as the less constrained baselines, and secondary-task fulfillment is lower (reported A/B/C/D: 100/58/39/27%).
- **Physical experiment:** A UR5 v3.2 on a powered 1.55 m linear rail executes shared-workspace Cartesian motion. Joint commands are sent at 125 Hz; the rail actuator runs at 1 kHz EtherCAT; obstacle observations are resampled to the IK at 30 Hz. In the reported human-avoidance run, the normalized deviation satisfies `max_i |Δq_i/Δq_max_i| ≤ 1` (Fig. 5).
- **Model/limits:** The rail speed limit is 0.5 m/s and its acceleration limit is 1 m/s². The arm velocity limit is 1.4 rad/s, and joint acceleration limits are 1.57 rad/s². The physical run uses `Δq_max=0.4`, `Δq_ref=0.3`; the simulation uses `0.2` and `0.15`, respectively.

## Relevance and evidence boundary

This is strong adjacent evidence for enforcing trajectory-consistent joint deviation under joint movement limits, including a physical experiment. The method is a local differential QP controller that depends on a supplied nominal trajectory. It does not label or enumerate IK branches, analyze or guarantee a singularity transition, or establish periodic IK closure. The paper's collision-free nominal path is generated upstream; the deviation tube itself is not proven collision-free for every configuration inside it. Viability is for the modeled double integrator and a polygonal approximation, so it is not a general certificate for arbitrary robot dynamics or tracking error. Because the experimental platform includes a moving prismatic rail, it should not be counted as fixed-base hardware validation.
