# Full-text reading note — Real-time optimized inverse kinematics of redundant robots under inequality constraints

## Bibliographic identity and source

- Linlin Zhang, Huibin Du, Z. Qin, Yue-Jing Zhao, and Guang Yang, Scientific Reports 14, 29754 (2024), DOI [10.1038/s41598-024-81174-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-81174-8).
- Read the publisher Version of Record at [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-81174-8).

## Research question and assumptions

Provide fast velocity-level IK for a redundant manipulator while satisfying inequality constraints in online tasks. Evaluations use a fixed-base 7-DOF KUKA iiwa; the main task is local tracking/control, not selecting a branch over a full prescribed path.

## IK representation

Each velocity update is a constrained linear program (LP), reducing unknowns to task scaling and null-space/free variables. An iterative revised-simplex procedure is used instead of a quadratic program.

## Solution-branch treatment

Redundancy is resolved locally at each update. The method does not label IK branches, guarantee persistent branch identity, or provide a branch-transition planner.

## Singularity handling

No explicit passage through a singularity or branch switching is studied. Inequality-limited local control is not a singularity-transition method.

## Constraints and executable feasibility

Joint range, velocity, acceleration, and model-based torque inequalities are included. Obstacle avoidance uses a one-step linearized distance condition for selected control points (e.g., elbow/wrist) relative to a threshold obstacle. This is a local approximate point constraint, not a whole-body swept-volume or continuous-clearance certificate. A deviation-enabled formulation may relax task accuracy when constraints make the nominal task impossible.

## Guarantees

The LP update enforces formulated inequalities under its model and feasibility assumptions. It does not prove a full Cartesian trajectory has a continuous joint realization or remains collision-free between updates.

## Experiments and reported results

Simulation uses KUKA iiwa paths and obstacle planes with a 50 mm distance threshold; hardware trials use a real KUKA iiwa with FRI, including sensor-driven tasks. One physical comparison without obstacle constraints contacts a physical box; obstacle-constrained experiments use selected link points and simplified geometry (Secs. 4–5). LP is compared with QP/SNS-style methods for online operation.

## Authors' limitations

The obstacle model constrains selected points rather than proving that the whole arm remains clear of finite obstacles. The control is local and does not resolve branch identity or whole-path proof.

## Evidence-bounded TCIK classification

Strong adjacent evidence for local IK with joint/velocity/acceleration/torque inequalities and approximate obstacle avoidance, including hardware. It does not establish branch × singularity handling or whole-path collision/trajectory feasibility.
