## Research question and assumptions

The paper targets the non-cyclicity of damped least-squares (DLS) inverse kinematics: a closed, repeated end-effector path can produce joint-space drift in a redundant manipulator, including drift of the elbow swivel angle. The proposed controller uses a fixed starting joint configuration `q_s` as the reference while solving each target pose. The studied mechanisms are serial, fixed-base redundant arms: a modified 9-DoF KUKA iiwa model in simulation and a 7-DoF KUKA iiwa 14R820 on hardware.

## Method

The standard iterative DLS update uses `J* = Jᵀ(JJᵀ + λ²I)⁻¹`. The proposed position-level update is

`q_(k+1) = q_s + (I − κN)(q_k − q_s) − J*(x_k − x_g)`.

Here `q_k` is initialized from current joint feedback, `x_g` is the target end-effector pose, `N` is a null-space projection, and `κ` controls the attraction toward `q_s` in the redundant self-motion component. The paper computes `N` from a Jacobian SVD. It reports extra computational cost over DLS from the null-space term and says the SVD can be reused to reduce that overhead.

The stability analysis forms a linearized error state from end-effector and joint deviations, then uses the SVD-based decomposition to show eigenvalues within the unit interval for `0 ≤ κ ≤ 1`. This is a stability result for the paper's iterative formulation and kinematic linearization; it does not establish a general global theorem for all nonlinear paths, branch changes, or constrained hardware executions.

## Evaluation and results

- **Simulation:** A modified iiwa with 9 DoF follows a 200 mm end-effector circle while keeping orientation fixed. Both solvers run at about 200 Hz with `λ²=0.1`; the proposed method uses `κ=0.5` and `ε=10⁻¹⁵`. The legacy DLS joint angles drift. The proposed scheme is nearly cyclic; after each two-second end-effector cycle, reported return errors are `3.2×10⁻³ rad` for joint 3, `0.135×10⁻³ rad` for joint 4, and `2.2×10⁻³ rad` for joint 5. The paper also plots position/orientation tracking errors and describes the numerical path error as negligible (about `10⁻¹⁵` in its conclusion).
- **Physical test:** A fixed-base KUKA iiwa 14R820 tracks a 150 mm circle in a plane parallel to the base YZ plane with fixed end-effector orientation. Joint angles are streamed from MATLAB through KUKA Sunrise Toolbox. The legacy DLS solution drifts from the initial configuration; the proposed method returns close to it. A laser tracker samples the flange reflector at 100 Hz: the fitted circle radius is 150.13 mm and radial deviation from that fit is ±0.2 mm; the reported X-coordinate errors are within ±0.15 mm. The authors attribute physical error to model/DH mismatch, servo tracking, thermal error, gear inaccuracy and backlash.
- **3D-printing use case:** The authors describe elbow swivel-angle drift during repetitive printing with legacy DLS as a collision/print-quality concern; the proposed scheme is applied to suppress that drift. This section is a practical use case, but it provides no separate quantitative print-quality or collision-rate comparison.

## Relevance and evidence boundary

This is core evidence for repeatable joint trajectories under closed end-effector paths on a fixed-base redundant serial arm. It controls redundant self-motion by anchoring the configuration to the start of the cycle. That addresses cyclic drift and predictable posture; it is not explicit enumeration/tracking of discrete IK branches and does not study branch switching at a singular configuration. The paper claims DLS singularity immunity and presents a case where redundancy allows a path that becomes singular when one redundant axis is fixed. This supports regularized numerical robustness and redundancy use, but it is not a systematic singularity-transition experiment or proof of branch continuity through singularities.

No hard joint-position, velocity, acceleration, torque, or collision constraints are imposed. The null-space return term does not itself guarantee joint-limit or collision feasibility. The periodic test demonstrates approximate cycle closure under the reported setup; it does not report a general closure error bound for arbitrary closed task paths.
