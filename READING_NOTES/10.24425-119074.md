## Research question and task definition

The paper compares two algorithms for repeatable inverse kinematics: given a closed task-space path `x(s)`, an entry configuration `q(0)` is supplied and the method must construct a configuration-space loop `q(s)` satisfying `k(q(s))=x(s)` and `q(0)=q(smax)`. The use case is repetitive robotic work, where closure in joint/configuration space allows the same cycle to repeat without rechecking a new entry posture each time.

The evaluation uses fixed-base planar serial pendula with revolute joints, 3R and 5R, and a 2D end-effector position task. Link lengths are all one. The task loops are circles or axis-aligned rectangles; the paper assumes unlimited joint motion to simplify the reachable task space. It does not time-parameterize the path or model a physical robot.

## Compared methods

**Method 1 — augmented Jacobian.** The kinematics are extended with `r=n−m` artificial coordinates so the redundant problem becomes locally nonredundant. A polynomial `k_add(p,q)` is fitted so rows of the augmented Jacobian approximate a specially constructed orthonormal augmentation whose inverse reproduces the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse locally. The article compares linear and quadratic polynomial forms. It iteratively adapts a configuration-space region to the current Q-loop, optimizes the polynomial parameters over sampled configurations, then follows increasing task parameter `s` with Newton IK and interpolates the resulting configurations. This implicitly selects a local inverse sheet; it does not enumerate or label discrete IK branches.

**Method 2 — elastic-band continuation.** The discrete loop is initialized as `L_disc={(0,q(0)),(smax,q(0))}` and linearly interpolated, so its two endpoints remain the same configuration. The method computes the integrated task-space mismatch between the prescribed loop and the forward-kinematic image of the current Q-loop. If the error exceeds tolerance `ρ`, it finds the task point with the largest mismatch, brackets it by neighboring configurations, and initializes Newton IK at their parameter-weighted interpolation. A null-space secondary objective minimizes the sum of squared distances to both neighbors, limiting stretch of the current Q-loop. The new `(s*,q(s*))` sample is inserted and the process repeats until the integrated error is below `ρ`.

The elastic-band construction explicitly maintains cycle closure at the two endpoints and incrementally follows a configuration-space path from the supplied entry posture. That is pathwise continuation, not discrete branch labeling. The article states convergence when the task-space error decreases monotonically; the guarantee concerns the stated loop approximation procedure and does not certify the exact interpolated image between every finite set of samples beyond its error criterion.

## Singularity behavior and constraints

Both methods use Newton IK based on the task Jacobian, whose ordinary singular configurations satisfy `rank(J(q))<m`; the Newton solve may fail near such a configuration. The paper states that repeatability for an augmented inverse is guaranteed only if the trajectory avoids singular configurations. Method 1 has the additional augmented-Jacobian singular condition `rank(J_aug(q))<n`, so it introduces algorithmic singularities beyond those of the original task. Method 2 does not introduce these extra singularities and inverts an `m×m` matrix rather than the `n×n` augmented matrix. It is therefore more tolerant of singular configurations in the comparison, but neither method defines a transition rule through a singular set or proves that it can cross one.

There are no explicit joint-position, velocity, acceleration, torque, or collision constraints. “Unlimited motion at joints” is an evaluation assumption, so the Q-loop results do not establish feasibility for a bounded industrial arm.

## Evaluation and results

- **Protocol:** eight task setups vary 3R/5R redundancy, circle/rectangle geometry, and path size. Multiple entry configurations `q(0)` are sampled per setup; a mean and standard deviation of Q-loop length and runtime, shortest loop, and failure count are reported. Newton updates are limited to 3° per iteration; the stopping residual is `δ=0.005`. The code ran in Mathematica on an Intel Core2 2.66 GHz computer.
- **Reliability and speed:** the elastic-band method has zero failed tasks in all eight setups. The linear augmented method fails 2–404 tasks by setup; the quadratic augmented method fails 5–404. Elastic-band mean runtimes range from 0.042 to 0.11 s, versus 0.09–0.95 s (linear) and 0.42–1.54 s (quadratic). Runtime comparisons are specific to this old platform and implementation.
- **Q-loop length:** in the 5R rectangular Task 8, elastic-band mean length is 241°, standard deviation 31.2°, and best 154°. Linear augmentation gives 248.5°, 36.4°, and 156°; quadratic gives 252.4°, 35.6°, and 164°. The shortest solutions are close, while rare runs make Method 1 more than 60° longer. Starting configuration strongly affects the resulting loop length, increasingly so with higher redundancy.
- **Method tradeoff:** quadratic approximation does not improve loop quality over linear approximation in the reported comparisons, but is slower and has more failures in several tasks. The elastic-band method is easier to implement, has fewer failures, and its runtime scales more predictably with task-loop length.
- **Figures checked:** the planar robot models, Q-loop path examples, and Task 8 loop-length-difference histogram (Figs. 1–3) were visually inspected against the equations, caption, and Table 1.

## Limitations and relevance to this review

This is **core evidence** for configuration-space cycle closure in trajectory-level/repeatable IK. It formalizes the distinction between a closed Cartesian loop and a closed joint-space loop, and compares two ways to produce one. The elastic-band method gives the clearest constructive closure invariant because both loop endpoints are fixed at the supplied `q(0)` throughout insertion. It does not provide explicit discrete branch labels or a singularity-crossing policy, and all reported tasks are simulation-only planar 3R/5R position paths.

The singularity result is comparative: Method 2 avoids the additional algorithmic singularities introduced by the augmented-Jacobian method. It does not remove the original rank-loss set, guarantee bounded conditioning, or certify a solution through a singular transition. The absence of joint limits is a major transfer limit for fixed-base industrial serial manipulators with finite ranges. The paper’s runtime and success statistics depend on sampled entry postures, thresholds, and the stated numerical implementation; they are not a proof of completeness for arbitrary loops.
