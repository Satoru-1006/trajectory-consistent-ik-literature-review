## Question and formulation

The paper asks how to follow a sampled Cartesian end-effector path while minimizing the number of times the robot must stop and reconfigure. It explicitly permits configuration breaks. Therefore its objective is not to guarantee one uninterrupted joint-space lift of the whole Cartesian path. It optimizes the number of reconfiguration events first, then joint-space path length as a tie-breaker.

At each Cartesian waypoint the method constructs a finite candidate set of IK solutions (the reported experiments use 300 candidates per waypoint). It uses seeded Trac-IK for an initial candidate and greedily propagates RelaxedIK solutions between waypoints to obtain smooth candidates. DBSCAN groups near-duplicate solutions using a 0.05-rad joint-space threshold; random IK samples replenish the set. The authors observe that greedy propagation alone collapses candidate diversity.

## Method and guarantees

Dynamic programming connects the per-waypoint solution sets. Each state stores the smallest reconfiguration count, predecessor, and accumulated joint-space length. A transition calls `CHECK_CONT` on adjacent candidate configurations and the time interval. A failed continuous transition increments the reconfiguration count. The optimization is lexicographic: first minimize event count, then path length.

The candidate configuration identity is a sampled IK solution, not a persistent analytic branch label. The method does not derive a singularity-transition model or prove that the sampled graph contains every feasible branch. Continuity is decided by the implemented solver/check, and the candidate IK samples are accepted within 1 mm position and 0.01 rad orientation error. Thus DP optimality applies to the finite candidate graph and its transition oracle, not to the continuous IK manifold as a whole.

Joint-position bounds are enforced by the IK solver. A reconfiguration is associated with a joint-velocity threshold over a short transition interval, but the paper does not provide general hard velocity/acceleration/jerk or torque guarantees for the tracking segments. Collision and robot-dynamics guarantees are not established for all transitions. In the physical welding demonstration, RRT* plans a repositioning motion after the IKLink schedule chooses a reconfiguration; the event itself is not jointly optimized for route length, time, or collision clearance.

## Evaluation and evidence

The evaluation contains 70 trajectories: ten randomized paths each on iiwa, Sawyer, Panda, and UR5, plus ten Sawyer trajectories each for welding, screw tightening, and valve turning. IKLink achieves equal or fewer reconfigurations than the compared greedy and MultiGIK baselines in these cases. Mean event counts include: randomized iiwa 0.40 vs 13.80 (GreedyIK) and 1.90 (MultiGIK); Sawyer 1.10 vs 14.20 and 1.50; Panda 1.70 vs 16.60 and 4.40; UR5 1.50 vs 16.00 and 2.10. For welding, screw tightening, and valve turning, IKLink reports 0.90, 4.60, and 2.30 events respectively.

The authors time-match MultiGIK with 30 repetitions. The Python prototype is computationally expensive: reported random-trajectory runtimes are about 166.55 s (iiwa), 119.25 s (Sawyer), 219.92 s (Panda), and 121.87 s (UR5). The paper reports a Sawyer welding demonstration with one selected reconfiguration and RRT* repositioning. The evidence is simulation across four robot models plus a physical demonstration, not a real-time performance or continuous-feasibility certification.

## Authors' limitations and review assessment

The authors note that the objective treats all reconfigurations as equally undesirable; limited-range revolute joints make candidate sampling harder; solution quality and compute cost depend on IK sampler diversity; and the prototype is not optimized. They identify joint optimization of tracking and reconfiguration motion as future work.

**Relevance:** strong adjacent/core evidence for trajectory-level organization of multiple IK solutions and explicit reconfiguration events. **Boundary:** it intentionally permits breaks, so it does not solve the review's strict trajectory-consistent, uninterrupted branch-tracking problem. It does not guarantee singularity-safe transitions or full hard joint-derivative constraints.
