## Research question and assumptions

The paper asks how to compute local joint velocities for a redundant manipulator while tracking a desired end-effector position/orientation and managing multiple auxiliary tasks, especially obstacle clearance and joint angle limits, within a short sampling period. It uses the fixed-base 7-DoF ROMAN 7 serial manipulator. The task is sequential point-to-point tracking along a square welding path around a cylindrical obstacle; axis 7 yaw is ignored when it does not affect end-effector position.

## Method

The method modifies configuration control rather than adding tasks through a null-space projector. It minimizes a weighted quadratic cost over primary task error, auxiliary task error, and joint-velocity magnitude. A positive joint-velocity weight matrix makes the optimization matrix invertible without the task-Jacobian pseudoinverse used by the compared baseline.

Obstacle clearance uses cylinder/ellipsoid approximations, a nearest robot–obstacle point, a distance-threshold repulsive force, and the point Jacobian to convert that force to a joint-velocity term. Joint angle limits enter through an angle-dependent function `ζ_v,i = μ_v(θ_i−θ_mid)/((θ_i−θ_min)(θ_max−θ_i))`, which drives motion away from limits as they are approached. The resulting weighted update trades trajectory tracking against auxiliary tasks rather than enforcing exact task priority. Parameters are selected empirically; the article gives ranges/weighting guidance for orientation and additional tasks.

## Evaluation and results

- **Setup:** C++ controller on an RTX64 system, ELMO drive, and CAN communication. The experiment starts near a singular posture (all joint angles approximately `0.0001 rad`), moves to the welding start point, then follows a square path around a cylinder. Sample time is 2 ms and point tolerance is 0.01 mm. The paper describes a trajectory-control plant that also uses a fractional-order terminal sliding-mode controller with a sliding perturbation observer.
- **Conventional null-space baseline:** The simulation follows the path and avoids collision, with maximum path error reported as `−1.62 μm`, but axes 4 and 6 exceed their joint limits. Average computation time is 0.2103 ms per calculation.
- **Proposed configuration control:** In simulation, it avoids the obstacle and respects tested joint angle limits, with maximum path error 1.31 μm, no observed abrupt velocity change, and average calculation time 0.1573 ms. In the physical ROMAN 7 experiment, the authors report obstacle clearance, no collision, no abrupt velocity change in this run, and all calculations within 0.1 ms (within the 2 ms sampling interval).
- **Limits of that result:** The conclusion says velocity chatter was observed in extreme conditions, including obstacles and near joint limits; stable motion was observed away from such extremes. Parameter gains were chosen by trial and error. The authors identify possible local minima and propose future work on neural-network gain selection.

## Relevance and evidence boundary

This is high-quality, fixed-base hardware evidence for online joint-limit and obstacle handling during sampled Cartesian trajectory tracking. It is adjacent evidence for trajectory-consistent IK: it outputs a local differential joint-velocity solution, but does not label or track discrete IK branches, analyze a singularity transition, or enforce cycle closure. The angular limit term is a repulsive/regularizing function in a weighted optimization, not a hard inequality-constrained IK certificate. Likewise, the tested clearance is empirical under the paper's simplified obstacle models; it is not a proof that every continuous motion or all robot links remain collision-free under model and tracking uncertainty.
