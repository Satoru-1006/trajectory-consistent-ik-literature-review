## Research question and contribution

The paper asks how to time-parameterize a prescribed NURBS end-effector path quickly while satisfying redundant-manipulator joint motion limits. It converts limits observed in a trial joint trajectory into a path-dependent end-effector velocity-limit curve (VLC), then divides the path at VLC minima and applies a seven-phase asymmetric jerk-limited S-curve to each segment. The phase durations are optimized with the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA).

The contribution is path-velocity scheduling under joint position, velocity, and acceleration bounds, including a real-robot tracking experiment. It is not an IK branch-enumeration or branch-transition algorithm; its trajectory planner relies on an upstream closed-loop IK routine for the trial joint motion.

## Robot, task, and formulation

- The model and hardware are fixed-base, 9-DoF serial redundant manipulators with nine revolute joints. The Cartesian task is a specified NURBS path with a fixed end-effector orientation in the reported diamond simulation and butterfly experiment.
- The end-effector path parameter is advanced iteratively from the scheduled path speed; arc length over NURBS intervals is approximated with composite Simpson integration (Eqs. 26–32). A closed-loop numerical IK method from the authors’ earlier work maps a trial end-effector profile to joint positions.
- The seven-phase profile has three acceleration phases, a constant-speed phase, and three deceleration phases (Sec. 2.1, Fig. 2). The optimization variables are the seven phase durations. Boundary displacement and end-speed conditions, end-effector speed/acceleration limits, and phase jerk conditions define feasibility. Four profile cases allow phases to have zero duration for short paths and nonzero start/end speeds. WOA searches feasible durations and minimizes total traversal time (Algorithm 1, Eqs. 1–25).
- Joint position, velocity, and acceleration limits are written in Eq. 33. Given adjacent points on the trial joint trajectory, the method chooses a minimum time interval satisfying joint velocity and acceleration limits (Eqs. 34–37), using an interior-point nonlinear program. It updates end-effector speed at the path samples (Eq. 38); these values form the VLC used as a scheduling constraint (Eq. 39). The NURBS curve is segmented at VLC local minima, and each segment is scheduled independently (Algorithm 2).
- This is a sampled conversion based on the trial joint path and path interpolation interval. The paper’s “exact” VLC claim is not accompanied by a continuous-time bound between samples, a robustness margin, or a proof that a different IK branch would preserve the same limits. Position feasibility depends on the upstream redundant IK trial trajectory. The S-curve limits end-effector jerk; the paper does not impose a separate joint-jerk bound.

## Branch continuity, singularities, and constraints

- **Solution branch:** No branch labels, solution-set tracking, branch matching, continuation corrector, or switch rule is given. A closed-loop numerical IK routine generates prior joint samples, but its branch behavior is not analyzed or attributed to this paper’s new planner.
- **Singularities:** No singularity index, singularity classification, transition strategy, or deliberately singular path test is reported. The method is therefore not evidence for controlled passage between IK branches at a singular configuration.
- **Joint constraints:** The planner explicitly uses joint position, velocity, and acceleration limits to shape the end-effector VLC. The numerical constraint conversion is evaluated along sampled IK poses; the article does not establish continuous-time satisfaction between samples. It does not report joint jerk, torque, collision, or uncertainty constraints.
- **Time objective:** WOA chooses phase durations for each path segment. Constraint conversion and segmentation precede the segment-wise minimum-time search; the result is an offline trajectory rather than a receding-horizon IK controller.

## Evaluation and findings

**Simulation (Sec. 4):** A virtual 9-DoF manipulator tracks a diamond-shaped NURBS path at fixed end-effector orientation. The VLC divides the path into six segments. The comparison is with bidirectional look-ahead (BLA), traditional symmetric S-curve (TSS), and a trial profile based only on end-effector limits (TMP). TOAS takes 6.910 s, versus 8.055 s for BLA and 8.100 s for TSS; TMP takes 7.030 s but violates joint acceleration limits. In the simulation, mean position/orientation errors for TOAS are 5.37×10⁻⁵ m and 2.95×10⁻⁶ rad. TSS’s task-space profile appears similar but exceeds acceleration limits on joints 1, 2, and 6; TMP has larger tracking errors when its constraints are violated.

**Hardware (Sec. 5):** The authors use a 9-revolute-joint platform with a 200 Hz command loop, 1 kHz encoders, an 18 Hz velocity-loop bandwidth, and per-joint velocity/acceleration limits of 0.1 rad/s and 0.5 rad/s². A 1.62 m butterfly NURBS path is divided into 13 VLC-based segments. TOAS completes it in 96.755 s, compared with 113.195 s (BLA) and 113.055 s (TSS). Mean position and orientation errors for TOAS are 7.92×10⁻⁴ m and 3.24×10⁻⁴ rad; BLA is slightly more accurate on these two reported means. The experiment supports practical trajectory execution and the reported time reduction under the selected setup, not a continuous-time or branch-continuity guarantee.

The evidence includes both virtual simulation and physical manipulator execution. The paper reports pointwise tracking-error statistics and joint motion plots, but not branch-switch counts, singularity margins, inter-sample constraint proofs, repeated-trial distributions, or a direct test of the IK solver across singularity transitions.

## Author-stated limitations and review relevance

The authors explicitly describe TOAS as offline and dependent on a set of trial joint trajectories used as priors for imposing joint limits in operation space. They identify real-time treatment of joint velocity and acceleration bounds as future work.

**Full-text synthesis role: adjacent evidence for joint-constrained path time-scaling, with a useful hardware validation.** It is directly informative about velocity/acceleration limits shaping a Cartesian path speed profile for a redundant fixed-base serial robot. It does not demonstrate trajectory-consistent branch tracking, singularity crossing, or branch continuity, and should not be cited as evidence for those mechanisms. The reported feasibility is tied to the chosen sampled trial IK path and execution setup.
