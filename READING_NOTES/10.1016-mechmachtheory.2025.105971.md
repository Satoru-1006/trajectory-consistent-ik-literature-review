# Full-text reading note — IK-Geo: Unified robot inverse kinematics using subproblem decomposition

## Bibliographic identity and source

- Alexander J. Elias and John T. Wen, Mechanism and Machine Theory 209 (2025), article 105971, DOI [10.1016/j.mechmachtheory.2025.105971](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mechmachtheory.2025.105971).
- Read publicly exposed author-uploaded journal full text at [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390960433_IK-Geo_Unified_robot_inverse_kinematics_using_subproblem_decomposition). The article header identifies the journal, volume, article number, authors, and DOI. Publisher metadata lists open access; its PDF was not compared line by line.

## Research question and assumptions

Provide an efficient unified geometric IK solver for broad families of fixed-base 6R arms and extend it to 7R arms through a redundancy parameter. The central problem is returning pose-level IK solutions; path examples illustrate how solutions can be connected.

## IK representation

Decomposes IK into six geometric Paden–Kahan-style subproblems. Cases with three consecutive intersecting/parallel axes admit closed form; many other industrial structures reduce to a one-dimensional search and general 6R geometry to a two-dimensional search. For 7R robots, fixing one joint reduces the problem to 6R. A tangent-half-angle polynomial route is presented for stronger all-root behavior.

## Solution-branch treatment

The solver returns multiple/all candidate branches for a pose and handles continua of solutions in degenerate cases. Search mode can use an initial guess to find a nearby root, but the contribution is an IK solver, not a persistent branch tracker. Example paths plot candidate solutions over time.

## Singularity handling

Subproblem solutions include singular/continuous cases; least-squares and continuous approximate solutions can remain numerically continuous at workspace boundaries. A 200-sample ABB IRB 6640 example traverses boundary and internal singularities and resolves a continuum using the specified singular direction (Secs. 4.3–4.4). This is a numerical path demonstration, not a general constrained singularity-crossing theorem.

## Constraints and executable feasibility

Search can be restricted to joint intervals, but the paper does not formulate whole-path velocity, acceleration, jerk, torque, collision, or clearance constraints. There is no whole-path trajectory optimizer or proof of constrained execution.

## Guarantees

The polynomial method is presented as stronger on root completeness than the search implementation; root completeness is distinct from selecting a continuous, constraint-feasible branch over a path. The search is numerical and sampling can miss roots. Path examples do not certify inter-sample feasibility.

## Experiments and reported results

Runtime comparisons use randomized robot-family poses on an Intel i7-3770K/16 GB platform. Closed-form MEX/Rust solutions are reported below 6 microseconds; search-based methods take longer and vary by robot (Sec. 6, Tables 4–5). Path illustrations include an ABB IRB 6640 with 200 samples and singularity cases. No physical robot experiment is reported.

## Authors' limitations

The conclusion calls for implementation optimization, better search strategies, and further applications beyond 6R. Whole-trajectory planning is not claimed.

## Evidence-bounded TCIK classification

Strong foundational evidence for geometric multiple-solution IK, explicit branches, and singular/continuum solutions, useful alongside path-level methods. It does not establish branch × singularity × hard-constraint path feasibility.
