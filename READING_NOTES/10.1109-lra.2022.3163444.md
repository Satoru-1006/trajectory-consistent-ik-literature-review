# Full-text reading note — Globally Optimal Solution to Inverse Kinematics of 7DOF Serial Manipulator

## Bibliographic identity and source

- Pavel Trutman, M. El Din, D. Henrion, and T. Pajdla, IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (2022), DOI [10.1109/LRA.2022.3163444](https://doi.org/10.1109/lra.2022.3163444).
- Read matching-title arXiv author preprint [arXiv:2007.12550v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12550), posted 2020. It matches the DOI title, but the 2022 Version of Record was not compared line by line; claims below are bounded to the preprint.

## Research question and assumptions

Find a globally optimal pointwise IK configuration for a 7R pose under joint-position bounds, minimizing weighted deviation from a preferred configuration. The tested model is a fixed-base KUKA LBR iiwa; poses are sampled independently rather than followed along a prescribed path.

## IK representation

The authors formulate trigonometric IK as polynomial/semi-algebraic optimization and apply Lasserre moment/SOS relaxations. A degree-reduced formulation reduces relaxation cost.

## Solution-branch treatment

The objective selects a configuration relative to a reference preference at each pose. It does not assign persistent branch identifiers, continue one branch along a path, or plan branch changes. Multiple pointwise candidates are not branch tracking.

## Singularity handling

No explicit singularity detection, passage, or branch-transition controller is proposed. No pathwise singularity claim is evaluated.

## Constraints and executable feasibility

Joint-angle position limits are explicit inequalities. The formulation does not impose collision/clearance, velocity, acceleration, jerk, torque, or whole-path task constraints. It does not certify a continuous joint lift of a Cartesian path.

## Guarantees

The global-optimum claim applies to the pointwise polynomial problem when the chosen relaxation is tight and numerically solved; it is not a path-level guarantee. The degree-reduced order-2 procedure has failures on a small fraction of tested poses.

## Experiments and reported results

The preprint tests 10,000 randomly generated KUKA iiwa poses, including feasible and infeasible poses. It reports about 99% solve/infeasibility-certification success for the degree-reduced approach at relaxation order 2; median pose errors and solve times are tabulated. The method is positioned as offline rather than an online path tracker (Sec. IV and results tables).

## Authors' limitations

The paper discusses the high computational cost of moment/SOS methods and motivates degree reduction; the evaluated relaxation still has failures and is not positioned as online trajectory planning. No collision or trajectory-level evaluation is reported.

## Evidence-bounded TCIK classification

Adjacent evidence for globally optimizing a constrained, redundant single-pose IK objective. It does not establish branch identity, singularity passage, or executable whole-path feasibility.

## Source locators

- Polynomial formulation and optimization: Secs. II–III.
- Degree-reduced relaxation and convergence/failure behavior: Sec. III and Sec. IV tables.
- Random-pose evaluation and conclusion: Secs. IV and VIII.
