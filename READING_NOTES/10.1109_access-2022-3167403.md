## Research question and assumptions

The paper asks how to solve differential inverse kinematics for a redundant arm while enforcing joint position, velocity, and acceleration bounds. It uses the kinematic model of the fixed-base 7R KUKA LWR4+ and a 3D end-effector position task (orientation is not controlled in the reported scenarios). The desired Cartesian path is a sequence of linear segments with quintic time laws. Several Cartesian paths revisit the same task-space point, but the formulation does not require the joint vector at the end to equal its initial value.

## Method

The decision variable in the QP is joint acceleration `u_k = q̈_k`. The paper discretizes joint position and velocity with a constant-acceleration state update. It then imposes elementwise bounds on predicted position, velocity, and acceleration. Additional velocity bounds are derived from stopping-distance reasoning: if a joint is moving toward a limit, it must be able to decelerate before crossing it, under the assumed acceleration bound. These inequalities are converted to bounds on the current acceleration decision.

The task-velocity equality uses a Jacobian evaluated at an approximate next configuration `q̂_(k+1)`. That predicted configuration uses the previous QP solution to make the future-Jacobian constraint linear in the new acceleration. A closed-loop IK correction uses Cartesian position error. Secondary goals, such as minimizing joint velocity or acceleration, enter a weighted quadratic objective; those weights allow tradeoffs rather than strict task priority. The solver is MATLAB `quadprog` with its active-set method in the reported study.

This is a discrete-time constrained differential IK method. It does not label IK solution branches or define a branch-switch policy. It does not analyze or certify a singularity approach/crossing. Any local continuity comes from the feedback trajectory update and previous-step prediction; no branch-preservation or cycle-closure guarantee is stated. The acceleration variable and bound do not impose a jerk constraint.

## Evaluation and results

- **Setup:** Four numerical scenarios use the KUKA LWR4+ kinematic model, 5 ms sample time, MATLAB R2020b, an Intel i7-6500U, and MATLAB `quadprog` active-set. The comparison baseline is the classic Jacobian-pseudoinverse differential IK with CLIK correction. There is no physical robot experiment.
- **Scenario 1A, unconstrained:** Both methods track a five-segment position path. Maximum Cartesian error is reported as `5.62×10^-5 m` for classic IK and `1.67×10^-6 m` for QP IK. Average per-step compute time is 0.14 ms and 1.5 ms, respectively.
- **Scenario 1B, bounded joints:** Position bounds are ±120°, velocity bounds ±150°/s, and acceleration bounds ±250°/s². Classic IK violates acceleration bounds on joints 2 and 4; the QP trajectory reaches but stays within the plotted bounds. The maximum end-effector error remains `1.67×10^-6 m`; average QP time is 1.49 ms.
- **Scenario 2:** With position bounds ±100°, velocity bounds ±150°/s, and acceleration bounds ±350°/s², classic IK exceeds joint 1's position limit and also violates higher-level bounds shown in the plots. QP IK remains within the plotted bounds; maximum error is `1.95×10^-6 m` versus `6.82×10^-5 m` for classic IK. Average per-step times are 1.29 ms and 0.11 ms for QP and classic IK.
- **Scenario 3:** With bounds ±120°, ±150°/s, and ±350°/s², classic IK exceeds joint 2's velocity bound and violates acceleration bounds. QP IK satisfies the plotted bounds, with joint 2 reaching its velocity limit. Maximum error is `8.58×10^-6 m` versus `1.63×10^-4 m`; average per-step times are 1.18 ms and 0.09 ms.

Across the tested scenarios, the constrained QP was roughly an order of magnitude slower than the pseudoinverse baseline but remained under 2 ms per step on the reported computer. The authors infer that a more efficient low-level implementation and QP solver could support real-time use; this is a projection, not a measured real-time robot result.

## Limitations and relevance to this review

This is direct evidence for sampled, acceleration-decision differential IK with explicit joint position, velocity, and acceleration inequalities. The plotted simulations show the proposed solver honoring the constraints where the pseudoinverse baseline violates some of them. However, the kinematic equality uses a locally predicted Jacobian, the evidence is simulation only, and bounds are enforced in the discrete update; the paper does not prove between-sample continuous-time feasibility under model or tracking error. A constrained QP may be infeasible when the requested motion exceeds the robot's limits. The authors propose relaxing task velocity/acceleration or trajectory scaling in such cases and identify trajectory scaling as future work.

For the stated review, this paper is **core** on joint-bound handling in trajectory-level differential IK, but only adjacent on trajectory consistency: it has no explicit branch identity, branch transition, singularity passage, or joint-space loop closure. Its exact contribution is useful for constraint modeling, not as evidence that branches remain consistent through singular configurations.
